# Director's mathematical review of catalog entry 71

**Q1921. Internally reviewed, not peer reviewed.** Henigman and Karshon ask in Question 8.2 for existence on a closed connected symplectic manifold. Their Table 1 and the following discussion specify dimension six as the harder low-dimensional case. The construction answers the stated question in every even dimension at least eight. [Original source, §8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21159v1#S8.SS1)

I checked the dependency against Kotschick’s Theorem 2. It gives a closed connected symplectic six-manifold with a free symplectic circle action and contractible orbits. Both freeness and invariance of the form are necessary here. [Kotschick](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-005-0841-6)

Fix any integer m≥2. On P×S² the generator (ζ,R) and every nonidentity power act freely because the first factor action is free. The direct sum symplectic form is invariant under this finite group, hence descends uniquely through the covering and is nondegenerate locally. The first-factor circle commutes with the deck transformations. At a nonpole the stabilizer is trivial since a rotation power fixing a nonpole must have exponent divisible by m. At a pole it is exactly the subgroup of mth roots of unity by freeness upstairs. This proves effectiveness, local freeness and absence of fixed points. A Hamiltonian on the compact quotient would attain an extremum with zero differential, forcing the generating vector field to vanish there.

A regular orbit traversed once lifts to the complete circle upstairs, whose null homotopy projects down. At a pole the *primitive* circuit lifts only through 1/m of that circle and ends on a different sheet. A kth power closes upstairs exactly when m divides k. To conclude exact order m one also needs the other implication for the mth power: its closed lift is the entire upstairs orbit, already null-homotopic by Kotschick. This argument requires a connected regular cover, not that it be the universal cover. It applies to both poles for all m, including composite m. Multiplication by further symplectic spheres with trivial action carries the projected null homotopy for a regular orbit and leaves the exceptional order unchanged by projection and inclusion. The example has no fixed points and does not answer Question 8.1 or settle dimension six.

High confidence in this dependent covering-space proof. The finite check only corroborates the period bookkeeping. Original proof and catalog status are recorded separately from the bounded literature review.
