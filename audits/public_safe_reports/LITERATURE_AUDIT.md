## Exact dependency and nearest antecedents

- D. Kotschick, *Free circle actions with contractible orbits on symplectic manifolds*, Math. Z. 252 (2006), 19–25, Theorem 2, states existence of a closed symplectic manifold with a free symplectic circle action with contractible orbits in every even dimension ≥ 6. Its proof constructs a connected six-manifold directly as a principal circle bundle over a K3 mapping torus. It uses a primitive Euler class whose evaluation on spherical classes surjects onto $π_1(S^1)$. Accordingly, every upstairs fiber is trivial in $π_1$. It uses the construction of Fernández, Gray and Morgan (1991), specifically their Lemma 17 for the form, and provides its own choice of K3 data to obtain contractibility. This is exactly the packet's input. [Kotschick theorem and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0410243v3) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-005-0841-6) · [Fernández–Gray–Morgan publisher record](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/michigan-mathematical-journal/volume-38/issue-2/Compact-symplectic-manifolds-with-free-circle-actions-and-Massey-products/10.1307/mmj/1029004333.full)
- **Exact finite diagonal quotient precedent.** Álvaro Pelayo, *Symplectic actions of 2-tori on 4-manifolds*, Mem. AMS 204 (2010), no. 959, Example 2.8 in arXiv PDF p. 8, gives $(S^2 × T^2)/C_2$, with a half-turn of $S^2$, an antipodal translation on the first circle of $T^2$, a free diagonal deck action, and the symplectic form and $T^2$-action descended from the product. On restriction to that first circle the result has precisely the packet's two orbit types for $m=2$. The function $[s,x,y] ↦ 2x$ modulo 1 shows why the earlier example has no contractible circle orbits. A primitive principal orbit maps with degree 2 and a primitive exceptional orbit with degree 1. Both therefore have infinite order. The packet substitutes Kotschick's null-homotopic free orbits for the translating torus factor. Pelayo also supplies general diagonal associated-bundle models in Theorems 2.36 and 2.47 under the hypothesis that the torus orbits themselves are symplectic. One-dimensional circle orbits do not satisfy that hypothesis. Pelayo repeats the $C_2$-sphere example in his later expositions. [Pelayo Example 2.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0609848#page=8) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0065-9266-09-00584-5) · [2017 exposition, arXiv Example 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.09718#page=21) · [2015 exposition](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.06480#page=17)
- **Arbitrary isotropy order in a prior symplectic circle action.** Henigman's earlier classification, arXiv:2411.10157, Appendix A.1 on PDF pp. 62–63, describes the diagonal circle action of coprime slopes $(k,ℓ)$ on $T^2 × S^2$, with principal free orbits and two polar exceptional orbit types of stabilizer $C_k$. With $(k,ℓ)=(m,1)$ the orbit stabilizers coincide with those in the packet. Projection to the moving torus coordinate gives degree $m$ or 1 on the primitive orbits, so both have infinite order. The classification also provides existence for the specified admissible four-dimensional invariants. [Henigman Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.10157#page=62)
- **Smooth and orbifold precedents.** Henigman–Karshon Example 3.3 gives mixed orbit contractibility by a smooth equivariant connected sum with fixed points. Remark 3.6 explains that the constructed action cannot be symplectic. Classical Seifert fibrations already exhibit the underlying cover mechanism. For example, replacing the torus in Pelayo's construction by the Hopf $S^1$-space $S^3$, using the same diagonal $C_m$ action, directly gives a smooth five-manifold with the desired torsion pattern by the same lifts. This is an inference from elementary covering spaces. Godinho–Sousa-Dias's corrected fundamental-group theorem treats the fixed-point-free symplectic case via the regular level and explicitly distinguishes exceptional orbits. It is structural and does not assert the packet's realization. [Original §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21159v1#page=14) · [Scott on Seifert manifolds](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0108188) · [Godinho–Sousa-Dias erratum](https://www.math.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/~lgodin/Publications/Papers/CJMIIErratumFinal.pdf)
- **Six-dimensional leads.** The original paper lists Kotschick and Tolman in the all-contractible column without fixed points and McDuff, Tolman and Jang–Tolman in the all-contractible column with fixed points. Tolman's construction passes through orbifold reduced spaces, while her closed example has 32 fixed points. The closed examples constructed by Jang–Tolman have $2k$ fixed points for each $k ≥ 5$. These published main examples cannot serve directly as the requested locally free mixed examples. ## Checked orbit lifts and attribution

In the packet's smooth regular cover $P × S^2 → M$, writing $x=(p,s)$ and $g(p,s)=(ζp,Rs)$, the lifts of one primitive circuit are:

```text
nonpolar s, stabilizer 1         one reparametrized full fiber circuit
(p,s) ────────────────────────→ (p,s)      contracts upstairs

polar s, stabilizer C_m          one m-th of the full fiber circuit
(p,s) ────────────────────────→ (ζp,s) = g(p,s)
                                 different lift endpoint
m successive circuits           end at (p,s) and contract upstairs
```

Freeness on $P$ makes the diagonal quotient a manifold. An off-pole point gives trivial stabilizer, hence faithfulness. Every point has finite stabilizer, and compactness forces any real Hamiltonian to attain a maximum with zero infinitesimal generator. Together with the endpoint calculation this proves the asserted properties. The appropriate attribution is a direct adaptation of Pelayo's diagonal cyclic quotient using Kotschick's free symplectic action with contractible orbits, followed by the primitive-orbit lift calculation. The result answers the unrestricted question. Priority for this combination remains unknown.
